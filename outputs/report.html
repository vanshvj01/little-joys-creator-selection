Work with these five creators, and have each one film a real child tasting a Little Joys milk mix on camera, in English or Hinglish. In Little Joys' own ads, purchases from videos with a child tasting on camera were about 40% cheaper, after adjusting for product and timing.

Built from Little Joys' own data only: 100 ads, 20 creators and their 392 recent posts. Ad spend, purchases and revenue in this exercise are made-up numbers, treated here as if they were the real ad account.

Why nothing here says "proven"

With 99 ads, about 142 comparisons were tested. After allowing for that many tries, the best result still falls short of our bar for "strong" (q = 0.13, bar 0.10). "Suggestive" means the effect held after adjusting for product and timing, and still held with the single best ad removed. One ad, LJ-004 (₹140 per purchase), brought in 29% of all purchases. Without it, the child-tasting effect shrinks from 40% cheaper to 24% cheaper.

#1 Viha Dikonda @storiesbyvihaa

Proteinmix (in Viha's roti / paratha)
Why them
  • Viha is the presenter in 70% of posts — the child-led format behind LJ-004 (the account's best ad, ₹140 per purchase) and LJ-050.
  • Her single most-played recent post is Viha trying to speak Hindi in a Telugu accent (6.5M plays), so a Hindi script plays to an existing strength rather than against her voice.
  • She already films Viha eating and reacting to food (e.g. the restaurant spicy-food reaction, 3.3M plays).
  • Bonus signal (not scored): the audience file lists Little Joys among her past-year brands, and her audience is 30% women.
  • Followers 827,496; typical video reach 771k-3,096k plays (middle half of their last 16 videos).
Suggested hook

Viha, to camera, holding her plate, in her Telugu-accented Hindi: "Aaj dekho meri plate mein kya kya hai!" On screen: "Viha ki roti mein ek secret hai 🤫". Spoken and on-screen together (LJ-004 opened the same way; ads with a spoken + on-screen hook did better).

Format

30-35s Reel, 9:16, home setting, Hindi/Hinglish. Produce a Telugu cut of the same video as an A/B test.

Script outline
0-3sViha with her lunch plate, spoken + text hook (above).
3-10sCut to Mom's hands adding one scoop of Proteinmix to the atta; Viha peeks in suspiciously.
10-20sViha bites the roti, does her signature exaggerated chewing face, then: "Taste toh bilkul same hai!" This is the core moment — a real child genuinely tasting on camera.
20-30sMom voiceover (Hinglish): 12g plant protein in one scoop, from pea, brown rice and soy; no added sugar, no preservatives.
30-35sViha asks for a second roti; packshot with "Little Joys Proteinmix" on screen.
Claims to make
  • Proteinmix: 12g plant protein in one scoop (21 ads, ₹290 per purchase together; e.g. LJ-004, LJ-056, LJ-097)
  • Proteinmix: no change in taste or smell of roti / pancakes (8 ads, ₹170 per purchase together; e.g. LJ-004, LJ-056, LJ-097)
  • Proteinmix: made from pea, brown rice and soy protein (8 ads, ₹160 per purchase together; e.g. LJ-004, LJ-098, LJ-052)
  • No added sugar, no preservatives (16 ads, ₹480 per purchase together; e.g. LJ-056, LJ-020, LJ-048)
Avoid
  • An adult script read by Viha about nutrients (her profile: an adult reading marketing copy feels foreign).
  • Studio lighting; keep the home-video look.
  • Height / 'overall growth' promises and deficiency fear (both did worse in the ads).
  • Telugu as the only version — no Telugu ad has ever been tested; regional-language ads cost 2.0x as much per purchase.
Expected performance
  • No ad by this creator exists, so there is no direct measurement. The estimate comes from the 12 Little Joys ads built the same way (child reacts to taste on camera + English/Hinglish + milk-mix product).
  • Those ads cost ₹200 per purchase together (90% range ₹160-₹380), versus ₹410 for the whole account. Without the single best ad (LJ-004) it is ₹330 (₹280-₹450).
  • Individual ads in that group ranged from ₹140 to ₹730: execution matters as much as the recipe. Read this as 'likely at or better than the account average', not a forecast.
  • Organic reach: their typical video gets 771k-3,096k plays; too few sponsored videos to say whether sponsored posts get fewer plays.

How we know this

  1. Checked the data. 100 ads, ₹72.6 lakh spend, 16,856 purchases. Left out LJ-089 (₹4.3L spend, 1,544 add-to-carts, 0 purchases: probable tracking break). 3 videos had each run twice and 3 more scripts were near-copies, so the 99 ads count as 93 independent creatives.
  2. Compared ad ingredients on cost per purchase. Each comparison was adjusted for product and for the date the ad was switched off, and resampled 2,000 times to get the ranges. Every finding was then re-checked with the excluded ad put back and with the single best ad removed.
  3. Checked the AI labels by hand against transcripts and video frames: 149 of 157 checks agreed (95%, 95% range 90%–97%).
  4. Scored creators on fit with those ingredients, reach and safety, using the weights below, then re-ranked them 5,000 times with the weights shifted at random.
  5. Wrote briefs using only claims Little Joys has already run, each linked to the ads and posts it rests on.

Confidence and limitations

  • Small sample. 99 ads. No ingredient reaches "strong"; the top findings are "suggestive" and depend partly on one very successful ad (LJ-004).
  • No creator has run a Little Joys ad, so each creator's results are inferred from ads with the same ingredients (12 ads: ₹200 per purchase together, range ₹160–₹380; ₹330 without LJ-004; individual ads ₹140–₹730). Treat this as a direction, not a forecast.
  • Telugu is untested. No ad ran in Telugu or Gujarati. Ads in other regional languages cost 2.0× as much per purchase, so the #1 brief is written in Hindi, with a Telugu version suggested as a test.
  • Performance numbers are synthetic for this exercise; the patterns are only as real as the export they come from.
  • AI labels are mostly right (95% on our checks). The post-level language label was wrong for spoken language, so transcripts were used instead.
  • Audience data covers 3 of 20 creators. It is shown as background and does not affect any score.
  • Judgement calls are written down and editable: which brands count as competitors, the scoring weights, and the choice of ohmother.in over the model's #5.
