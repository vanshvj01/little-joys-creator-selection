Which five creators next, and what should they say?

Work with these five creators, and have each one film a real child tasting Proteinmix or Nutrimix on camera, in English or Hinglish. In Little Joys' own ads, videos with a child tasting on camera got purchases about 27% cheaper than comparable ads of the same product (95% range: 38% cheaper to 5% cheaper). That is a consistent pattern across our checks, not proof: the ad account is small.

Built from Little Joys' own data only: 100 ads, 20 creators and their 392 recent posts. Ad spend, purchases and revenue in this exercise are made-up numbers, treated here as if they were the real ad account.

Why nothing here says "proven"

With 99 ads, about 142 comparisons were tested. After allowing for that many tries, the best result still falls short of our bar for "strong" (q = 0.34, bar 0.10). "Suggestive" means the effect held after adjusting for product and stop date, held again when the launch date was used instead of the stop date, and still held with the single best ad removed. One ad, LJ-004 (₹140 per purchase), brought in 29% of all purchases. Without it, the child-tasting effect is 23% cheaper.

How much the label check matters for the headline

The AI tagged 39 ads as "child reacts to the taste". A person checked every one of them against the transcript and video frames: 26 confirmed, 4 wrong (removed), 9 unclear (a child drinks or eats, but no reaction is visible in the frames). The result barely moves:

#1 Viha Dikonda @storiesbyvihaa

Risk to check before booking

Her audience is only 30% women (70% men; largest age group 25-34, at 44%). The buyer is usually a mother, so a large share of her reach may never buy. This is the only one of the five with audience data, so it is shown as a risk, not scored. Ask for her current audience split before booking.

Why them
  • Viha is the presenter in 70% of posts — the child-led format behind LJ-004 (the account's best ad, ₹140 per purchase) and LJ-050.

How we know this

  1. Checked the data. 100 ads, ₹72.6 lakh spend, 16,856 purchases. Left out LJ-089 (₹4.3L spend, 1,544 add-to-carts, 0 purchases: probable tracking break). 3 videos had each run twice and 3 more scripts were near-copies, so the 99 ads count as 93 independent creatives.
  2. Read the product from the ad itself. The AI's product label filed 13 of the 18 Proteinmix ads under "other", next to sauces and spreads. Proteinmix is the cheapest product per purchase, so the product is taken from each ad's own text instead. This roughly halved some earlier effect sizes; every number here uses the corrected product.
  3. Compared ad ingredients on cost per purchase. Each comparison was adjusted for product and for the date the ad was switched off, and resampled 2,000 times to get the ranges, keeping re-used videos together so they never count twice. Every finding was then re-checked three ways: with the excluded ad put back, with the single best ad removed, and adjusting for launch date instead of stop date (ads are often stopped because they did badly, so the stop date partly reflects the result).
  4. Checked the AI labels by hand against transcripts and video frames, including every ad tagged with the headline label: 167 of 178 checks agreed (94%, 95% range 89%–97%). Wrong labels on the headline ingredient were corrected before the analysis.
  5. Scored creators on fit with those ingredients, reach and safety, using the weights below, then re-ranked them 5,000 times with the weights shifted at random.
  6. Wrote briefs using only claims Little Joys has already run, each linked to the ads and posts it rests on. Each claim is rated against ads for the same product, so a cheap product does not make its claims look good by itself.

Confidence and limitations

  • Small sample. 99 ads. No ingredient reaches "strong"; the top findings are "suggestive". One ad (LJ-004) brought in 29% of all purchases and dominates the Proteinmix numbers.
  • No ad in this data features any of the five, so each creator's results are inferred from ads of the same product built the same way. Proteinmix lookalikes: 7 ads, ₹170 per purchase together (range ₹140–₹420), ₹300 without LJ-004. Nutrimix lookalikes: 7 ads, ₹370 (range ₹290–₹540) against ₹490 for all Nutrimix ads. Treat these as a direction, not a forecast. @storiesbyvihaa has worked with Little Joys before; that ad cannot be identified in this export, so ask the brand team for its results.
  • Language. No ad ran in Telugu or Gujarati. Ads in other regional languages cost 47% more per purchase after adjustment, and the 10 pure-Hindi ads also did worse (not conclusive), so every brief is in English or Hinglish, with a Telugu cut of the #1 video as an optional side test.
  • Audience fit is mostly unknown. Only 3 of 20 creators have audience data. For @storiesbyvihaa it shows a mostly male audience, which is flagged on her card as a check before booking.
  • Performance numbers are synthetic for this exercise; the patterns are only as real as the export they come from.
  • AI labels are mostly right (94% on our checks), with two exceptions that were fixed: the product label (Proteinmix filed as "other") and the creator-post language label (it describes the caption, not the spoken language, so transcripts were used instead).
  • Judgement calls are written down and editable: which brands count as competitors, the scoring weights, and the choice of ohmother.in over the model's #5 (the two are close to a tie under shifted weights).
